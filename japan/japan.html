Ukiyo-e (浮世絵)

Ukiyo-e, or “pictures of the floating world,” are woodblock prints from the 17th century Edo period in Japan. This art form was a collective effort involving merchants, artists, publishers, and working people, giving Ukiyo-e a unique voice.

Despite being considered "low" art by some, Ukiyo-e's artistic and technical mastery was consistently remarkable. It offered cultural status to those outside the traditional domains of the shogunate, temples, and court

Ukiyo-e depicted both historical scenes and contemporary life, playing a crucial role in shaping Western perceptions of Japanese art in the late 19th century. Its vibrant colors, bold compositions, and unique perspectives influenced many Western artists.

Origami (折り紙)

Origami is the traditional Japanese art of paper folding, dating back to the Edo period. This craft transforms a simple sheet of paper into intricate designs and figures without cutting or gluing.

Origami is more than just a craft; it is a reflection of Japanese culture and creativity. From delicate cranes and frogs to complex geometric shapes, each piece showcases remarkable skill and precision.

The art of Origami has spread worldwide, influencing fields beyond art, including mathematics, engineering, and education. Its blend of simplicity and complexity continues to inspire and captivate people of all ages.
